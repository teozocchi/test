# 1. Brand philosophy

## The enemy — Idea

The "wellness" industry: fake organic labels, half-baked pseudoscience, and cheap products whose claims don't survive the math (e.g. €30 plastic shower filters sold as chlorine removers).

Notes:
- The €80B market size needs a source before it's used anywhere. **(Open)**
- The enemy explains *why we exist*; the products have to carry the brand. A brand that mostly debunks becomes a media channel, not a company.
- Our claims about competitors must meet the same standard as our claims about ourselves (see house rule 3). One overstated attack hands critics the win.

## Thesis — Proposed

> Industrial-grade hardware for human biology. We sell physics, thermodynamics and verifiable chemistry.

## House rules — Proposed *(Claude)*

1. **We only claim what we can measure, and we show the measurement.**
2. **We promise device performance, not health outcomes.** "Removes ≥95% of free chlorine at 10 L/min" is a physics claim we can prove. "Healthier skin" is a biology claim that would need clinical evidence. This rule also keeps us clear of EU health-claim and medical-device rules, which apply quickly once a product promises health effects.
3. **Claims about competitors meet the same standard as claims about ourselves:** measured, with the method published.
4. **Mistakes are corrected in public.** Retracted claims stay visible in the Claims Log, with the reason.
5. **If the honest answer is "you don't need this," we say it.** (E.g. Dutch tap water isn't chlorinated, so a chlorine filter is pointless there.)

## Name — Idea

"First Principle" is the working name.

- Risk: "first principles" is a heavily used phrase, so trademarks and search results will be crowded.
- Before committing: check EUIPO in the relevant classes, likely 9 (measuring instruments), 11 (water/air treatment apparatus) and 35 (retail).

## Slogan — Open

Candidates *(Claude)*:
- *Measured, not marketed.*
- *Don't believe us. Measure it.*
- *Physics, not promises.*
- *Show the math.*

## Aesthetic — Open

**Constraint:** engineering-led, but it must appeal to the mainstream. A full lab look, with every page reading like a datasheet, would confuse people.

**Proposed direction *(Claude)*: precise, not clinical.** On the surface, a premium consumer product: calm, warm, well photographed, closer to Braun or Apple hardware than to lab equipment. The engineering sits underneath, one tap away.

- **Palette:** material-led neutrals (metal, off-white, graphite). Safety orange at most as a small functional accent, e.g. marking where a measurement appears. Not the brand colour.
- **Type:** a clean sans-serif for everyday reading; monospace or a serif reserved for data and the rigorous layer.
- **Photography:** the product, and the measurement (e.g. a test strip before and after). People use the products normally. No glowing-skin shots.
- **Banned:** leaves, water droplets, glowing people, "detox", "toxins" without naming the molecule, "chemical-free".

## Signature mechanic — Proposed *(Claude)*

Ship a way to verify with every product. The filter comes with chlorine test strips; the CO₂ monitor explains why it uses a real NDIR sensor instead of a cheap sensor that only estimates CO₂. Customers checking our claims themselves is both the proof and the marketing.
