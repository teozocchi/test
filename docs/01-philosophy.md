# 1. Brand philosophy

## The enemy — Idea

The "wellness" industry: fake organic labels, half-baked pseudoscience, and products whose claims don't survive the math.

Notes:
- The enemy explains *why we exist*; the products have to carry the brand. A brand that mostly debunks becomes a media channel, not a company.
- Our claims about competitors must meet the same standard as our claims about ourselves (house rule 3). One overstated attack hands critics the win.
- **Don't size the enemy with the wellness-economy figure** *(Claude)*. The Global Wellness Institute valued the global wellness economy at $5.6 trillion in 2022, but that total includes gyms, healthy eating, personal care and wellness tourism. Most of it isn't pseudoscience, so "we're fighting a $5.6T industry" would overstate our own source. (It's also in US dollars, and a newer GWI edition may exist.) Market-size figures belong in the pitch material; see [go-to-market](03-go-to-market.md).

## Thesis — Proposed

> Industrial-grade hardware for human biology. We sell physics, thermodynamics and verifiable chemistry.

## House rules — Proposed *(Claude)*

1. **We only claim what we can measure, and we show the measurement.** Every claim, cited figures included, goes in the Claims Log.
2. **We promise device performance, not health outcomes.** "Delivers X m³/h of particle-free air" is a physics claim we can prove. "Better sleep" is a biology claim that would need clinical evidence. This also keeps us clear of EU health-claim and medical-device rules.
3. **Claims about competitors meet the same standard as claims about ourselves:** measured, with the method published.
4. **Comparisons include the competitors that beat us.** A chart showing only products we beat is marketing, not data.
5. **Mistakes are corrected in public.** Retracted claims stay visible in the Claims Log, with the reason.
6. **If the honest answer is "you don't need this," we say it.** A purifier does nothing for CO₂ (open a window), and in a room with already-clean air it won't do much.

## Tone — Decided

**Calm and clinical.** Combative sounds like a grifter; yelling about "the Matrix" sounds like a crypto scam. We don't insult competitors. We publish the measurement and let the data make the case.

Example: quietly posting a chart that compares the carbon in a Dyson with ours. Before a chart like that runs, it has to follow house rules 3 and 4; see "Comparisons" in [go-to-market](03-go-to-market.md).

## Aesthetic — Open

**Constraint:** engineering-led, but it has to appeal to the mainstream eventually. A full lab look, with every page reading like a datasheet, would confuse people.

**Tension to resolve:** "brutalist" now appears in two places (the purifier, and the website design). Brutalism reads well to the engineer-skeptic we're winning first, but it's polarizing for the mainstream buyers we want later.

A possible split *(Claude)*: let the **hardware** be heavy and industrial (steel, visible fasteners, stamped part numbers: the product's honest character), and keep the **website and packaging** calm and legible, "precise, not clinical." The brand can then reach the mainstream later without a redesign.

Proposed direction for the calm side *(Claude)*:
- **Palette:** material-led neutrals (metal, off-white, graphite). Safety orange at most as a small functional accent.
- **Type:** a clean sans-serif for everyday reading; monospace or a serif reserved for data and the rigorous layer.
- **Photography:** the product, and the measurement. People use the products normally. No glowing-skin shots.
- **Banned:** leaves, water droplets, glowing people, "detox", "toxins" without naming the molecule, "chemical-free".

## Signature mechanic — Proposed *(Claude)*

Give every customer a way to verify our claims:
- The purifier measures its own performance in the customer's room (the "room test" idea in [product](04-product.md)).
- We publish the test protocol, so anyone with a particle sensor can check our numbers independently.
