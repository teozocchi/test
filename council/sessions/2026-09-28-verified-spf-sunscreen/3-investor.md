# 3. The Investor

Council session 2026-09-28 · [Idea brief](idea.md) · [Believer's case](1-believer.md) · [Skeptic's attack](2-skeptic.md)

## Proof people will pay: none

- **Money collected: €0.** There's no page, no waitlist, no formula, no price and no audience. The purifier never launched, so the brand has no customers to start from.
- **The Believer shows that the problem exists, not that anyone pays to fix it.** The failed test tables, i+m's rebuttal and the INCIDecoder founder paying for two lab tests in 2020 all prove the problem. None of them is a customer paying a brand for a verified number.
- **People spend a lot on facial SPF:**
  - Prestige sun care in Europe sold €239.7m in the 12 months to March 2026, up 17%, on 10.2 million units. That's about €23.50 a unit. Face sun care grew 26% (Circana).
  - Beauty of Joseon's Relief Sun has sold 15 million units in total. BoJ's parent company, Goodai Global, reported ₩1.47 trillion of revenue in 2025 (about €0.9bn, my conversion), up 294%.
  - That money pays for texture, finish and price. BoJ publishes results from two labs and sells for €11–26.
- **Brands that sold on verification didn't get the money:**
  - After its scandal, Purito relaunched with results from two labs, in Korea and Italy. A review site describes its review volume as "moderate". BoJ sold on texture and became the number-one K-beauty sun care brand online.
  - Naked Sundays paused its Collagen Glow SPF 50+ in 2025 while it waited for new results. It then retested its whole range at two or more labs and now advertises that. I found no sales figures.
  - I found no brand selling "every batch, every raw result". Nobody owns that position, but nothing shows it earns anything either.
- **The Believer's best evidence is from Australia, and the money went to established brands.** Circana says Australian shoppers moved up to "trusted" SPF after the scandal:
  - the average price per unit rose 35% over four summers, from about A$11 to over A$15;
  - spending rose from A$64.4m to A$102.7m.

  Nothing shows any of it going to a new brand's self-paid lab reports.
- **The referee already gives buyers the verified number for free:**
  - Warentest rated dm's Sundance Med face fluid "gut" (1.8) and named it the price tip at €5.25 per 50 ml. 8 of the 14 face sunscreens it tested were "gut".
  - The EU problem is shrinking. Euroconsumers found 38% of facial sunscreens below their label in 2024, but only 3 of 44 new sunscreens failed in 2025.
- **Crowdfunding for small sunscreen brands is small.** Sun Slayer raised AU$12,926 from 197 backers. BEAME raised $6,306 from 134.
- **People do pay for the tests themselves, as donations.** Readers of Lead Safe Mama, a product-testing blog, have crowdfunded heavy-metal lab tests on hundreds of products since 2024. That pays for a report, not for a pricier tube.

## When the first real euro arrives

| Money | When | What it takes |
|---|---|---|
| Refundable pre-order | 1–7 days after a page goes up | A company, a way to take payments, and pre-order terms. It shows demand but isn't revenue. |
| First shipped tube, fastest route | April–May 2027, about 7 months away (estimate) | A contract manufacturer's ready-made formula that already has stability data, and all three volunteer-panel (in vivo) labs reading SPF 50 or more first time. Each in vivo test takes 4–12 weeks; the safety report and EU notification take 3–6 weeks. |
| First shipped tube, custom formula | Late 2027 at the earliest. The first full season is spring 2028, 18–20 months away (estimate). | 12–18 months of development (vendor sources) |

- **The first lab round is roughly a coin toss.** Take an honest "50+" formula, one whose true SPF is 60–70. The Skeptic's model gives it a 42–71% chance that all three labs read 50 or more.
  - I re-ran the model and got the same numbers.
  - Its spread of 0.27 is the between-lab standard deviation that the ALT-SPF ring study (Cole 2025) reports for in vivo testing, so the model uses the right input.
  - A failed round means printing "30" on the tube, or about six more months of development.
- **Miss April 2027 and you lose the year.** 60–68% of sun care sales fall in April–September, according to market-research summaries I couldn't check.
- **Cash out before that first euro:**
  - €65–165k for a custom formula and a 25,000-unit minimum order (the brief's estimate).
  - €15–90k for a ready-made formula and a run of 1,000–5,000 units (my estimate):
    - €7–40k for three in vivo labs;
    - €2–10k for lab-bench (in vitro), UVA and HDRS tests (HDRS has no published price);
    - €1–3k for the safety report and notification;
    - €2–10k for packaging artwork;
    - €3–25k for the tubes.

## What each tube earns

I priced it at €24.95 for 50 ml. That's about Circana's prestige average of €23.50, and above BoJ's fluid at dm (€18.95). After German VAT, the company keeps €20.97.

| Cost per tube | 25,000-unit batches | 5,000-unit batches |
|---|---|---|
| Making it (the brief's €2–4, not quoted; my estimate for small runs) | €2–4 | €3–5 |
| Three in vivo labs for every batch ($2.6–15k each, at about €0.90 per $) | €0.28–1.62 | €1.40–8.10 |
| Picking, packing and postage in Germany | €5–8 | €5–8 |
| Payment fees (estimate) | €0.70 | €0.70 |
| **What's left per tube (contribution)** | **€6.60–13.00** | **−€0.80 to +€10.90** |

- **The margin check depends on batch size.** Re-entry check 2 needs landed cost at no more than half the net price.
  - At €24.95 it passes with 25,000-unit batches.
  - With 5,000-unit batches at the top lab price, making and testing a tube costs up to €13.10 against a €10.48 ceiling, so it fails.
  - At €5.25, the price of the tube the referee already rated "gut", it fails at any batch size. The Skeptic is right on this.
- **At small scale, the testing promise can be the biggest cost.** "Every batch at three labs" costs up to €8.10 a tube, 39% of what the company keeps from each sale.
- **A lower price doesn't help.** At €14.95, just under BoJ, each tube leaves −€1.80 to +€4.60 even in 25,000-unit batches. That can't pay for a single customer, so the test has to run at about €25.
- **Customers bought with ads lose money in year 1:**
  - Published benchmarks for the cost of winning a customer (industry blogs):
    - $15–75 for a first beauty purchase from Meta ads;
    - $35–70 for online skincare brands;
    - €65–110 across all channels for European online brands, highest in Germany.
  - A customer who buys 2–3 tubes a year (the Skeptic's estimate at the amounts people actually apply) brings in €13–39 in year 1.
  - Buying all 8,300–12,500 customers that a 25,000-unit batch needs would cost €250k–1.4m at those rates (estimate).
  - If buyers apply the full test dose every day (the Believer's 6–8 weeks per tube), a customer is worth €40–104 a year and paid ads could work. Nothing in the file shows that they do.
- **Earning back the brief's €65–165k takes 4,300–15,500 tubes** (estimate). That's 1,400–7,700 customers who cost nothing to win, and 17–62% of the first 25,000-unit batch.

## The cheapest test this week (about €600)

Take full-price pre-orders, not a waitlist. For a €25 product, a small deposit proves too little.

1. **Day 1 (under €100): put up one Shopify page.**
   - Offer a 50 ml daily face fluid at €24.95, shipping April 2027, refundable on request until it ships.
   - Promise only the method: three independent in vivo labs, a label below the worst result, and every raw result for every batch.
   - If any lab reads below 50, the tube is labelled 30 and every buyer is offered a refund.
   - Give no SPF figure and make no claims about texture, because there's no formula yet.
2. **Show the products that beat you first:**
   - Sundance Med at €5.25 (Warentest "gut" and price tip);
   - Eucerin Oil Control (Warentest test winner);
   - La Roche-Posay UVMune 400;
   - BoJ (results from two labs published).

   Show each at that day's price, next to one sentence: "If you want a referee-verified SPF 50+ for the least money, buy the €5.25 tube." That's house rule 6, and it's what makes a pre-order placed next to it mean something.
3. **Days 2–7 (€500):** run Instagram and Facebook ads to skincare audiences in Germany, Austria, Denmark, Sweden and the Netherlands, and post in skincare communities that allow it. At $0.80–3.50 a click, €500 buys about 150–700 visits. Aim for 1,000–2,500 visitors in total, with the rest from unpaid posts (estimate).
4. **Day 7:**
   - fewer than 30 paid pre-orders: stop;
   - 30 or more: keep the page up to day 30, with total ad spend capped at €4,500.

This is the hardest week of the year to sell sunscreen: October is the low season, and ad prices peak in the last quarter. That's acceptable, because the buyer this brand needs wears SPF every day. Prestige SPF in the five biggest European markets grew 20% in January–March 2026, the darkest quarter (Circana).

## Would I put my own money in?

**No.**
- €0 has been paid, and there's no audience, formula or price.
- The margin only works at €20 or more. At that price, buyers pay for texture and brand, and no data shows anyone paying extra for verification. The referee's "gut" SPF 50+ face fluid costs €5.25.
- A customer is worth €13–39 in year 1, less than most published costs of winning one.
- The first euro that isn't refundable is at least 7 months away, and more likely 18–20. €15–165k goes out first. The first lab round fails an honest "50+" formula 29–58% of the time.

**The number that would change my mind: 300 paid, full-price pre-orders at €24.95 within 30 days**, with two conditions:
- they're collected on the page that shows the €5.25 Warentest tube first;
- they cost no more than €4,500 in ads in total, so €15 or less per buyer.

- That's 1.5 times Sun Slayer's backers and 2.2 times BEAME's.
- At €15 or less per buyer, a customer's second tube pays back what it cost to win them.
- 300 strangers paying 4.75 times the referee's price, for a tube with no texture, no referee verdict and a six-month wait, would be the proof of a price premium that the file can't show today.

At 300, I'd fund choosing a ready-made formula and one round of tests at three labs (€10–50k, estimate), but not stock. The first batch waits for the lab results.

## Figures checked

- **Confirmed:**
  - Sundance Med: €5.25 per 50 ml, rated "gut" (1.8), price tip, fourth of 14 (Warentest 04/2025). 8 of the 14 were rated "gut".
  - Eucerin Oil Control: 1.7, second of 14.
  - Circana's +26% for face sun care.
  - The brief's in vivo test price matches one lab's list price ($2,561–3,504). Vizor, a supplier of UV-filter ingredients, puts it at $5,000–15,000+.
  - Minimum orders of 1,000–10,000 units (vendor sources).
  - The Skeptic's 0.27 spread is the standard deviation the study reports, and the Skeptic's probability table recalculates exactly.
- **Not confirmed:**
  - Eucerin at €22.45: the results only showed other pack sizes.
  - BoJ at €18.95: dm does label Relief Sun "LSF 50", but the search results put €18.95 on the Aqua Fresh fluid and €25.95 on Rice + Probiotics. The lowest price online is €10.82.
- **New:**
  - The drop in Euroconsumers' failure rate, from 38% (2024) to 3 of 44 (2025).
  - Circana's figures on Australians paying more for trusted brands. They show trust moves money, but toward established brands.
