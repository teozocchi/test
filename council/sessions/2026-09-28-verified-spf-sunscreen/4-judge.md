# 4. The Judge

Council session 2026-09-28 · [Idea brief](idea.md) · [Believer's case](1-believer.md) · [Skeptic's attack](2-skeptic.md) · [Investor's assessment](3-investor.md)

## Verdict: KILL

Don't build it, and don't spend money testing demand for it. This kills the sunscreen candidate, not the brand. First Principle stays archived.

- **It fails re-entry check 3 on paper.** The check says: "No measured competitor already delivers that headline number for less" ([README](../../../README.md)).
  - Stiftung Warentest tested dm's Sundance Med Ultra Sensitiv fluid (SPF 50+). It rated it "gut" (1.8) and named it the price tip at €5.25 per 50 ml. 8 of the 14 face sunscreens in that test were rated "gut".
  - The screen passed check 3 "narrowly" only because it counted brands that publish their own lab data and left out the referee ([candidates](../../../docs/06-candidates.md)).
- **The project already wrote this rejection down:** "The honest product already exists, cheaply: … Stiftung Warentest's €100 purifiers." The last ruling set the bar for the next product in the same terms: "a category where measured competitors don't already own the headline number."
- **The promise works against the product.** The line is "Our label is lower than our worst test", kept for every batch at several labs. That ties the label to the lowest of a growing pile of results, and the lowest result can only fall.
- **Nothing shows that anyone pays for more verification.**
  - The money in facial SPF goes to texture, finish and price.
  - After Australia's scandal, the extra spending went to established brands.
  - The Investor found the EU failure rate falling. Euroconsumers found 38% of facial sunscreens below their label in 2024. In 2025, 3 of 44 new sunscreens failed.

### Why not FIX FIRST

The purifier got FIX FIRST because the case against it was a forecast that one cheap measurement could settle. Here the deciding measurement already exists. It's also the kind of evidence this brand trusts most: bought off the shelf, paid for by no brand, with the method published. The €5.25 tube plays the role here that the fan-selector result played for the purifier.

Nothing First Principle controls can change that:
- More labs, a better method or a stricter labelling rule would improve the brand's own evidence. None of them makes that evidence independent, and independence is what the referee's readers trust.
- The Investor's pre-order test is well built. But the re-entry rule reopens the project only for a candidate that passes all three checks on paper, and this one doesn't. Even 300 pre-orders would leave check 3 and the arithmetic below unchanged.

## The biggest risk

The referee already owns the number: Stiftung Warentest, which no brand pays, rated dm's €5.25 SPF 50+ face fluid "gut", and test results from labs a brand pays itself are weaker evidence, at nearly five times the price.

## Weighing the three

### The Skeptic wins on the evidence

- **The referee.** The Believer's customer buys by Warentest's verdicts. She trusts them because Warentest buys off the shelf and nobody pays it for a verdict. First Principle would offer samples it chooses, sent to labs it pays, which is how Lean Screen was certified. Warentest picks products mainly by how widely they sell, so a new brand won't get a referee verdict for years.
- **The ratchet.** I recalculated the Skeptic's model and got the same table. The model assumes lognormal results and a spread of 0.27 between labs, the figure from the ALT-SPF ring study.
  - A formula with a true SPF of 60 gets readings of 50 or more from all three labs 42% of the time. At a true SPF of 70, it's 71% (estimates).
  - One caveat: if the same three labs test every batch, later results are correlated, so the multi-year columns are rough guides, not exact figures. The first-round figures don't depend on that. And a lab that reads consistently low makes the problem permanent rather than occasional.
  - So the brief's "labelling below the worst result is easy" holds for one round of tests, not for every batch. The ways out are all bad:
    - a formula about twice as strong as its label. Nobody has quoted it, and it works against the light texture facial SPF sells on;
    - a "30" on the tube;
    - a results page that eventually shows the brand's own lab reading below the brand's own label.
- **The dilemma settles the brief's open disagreement.** The founder thinks lab variability is what consumer testers expose. Claude thinks it explains SPF 40 versus 50, not 4 versus 50. Whoever is right, the edge disappears:
  - if the spread is large, the ratchet bites;
  - if it's small, one test at any lab gives any brand the same confidence as First Principle's three, and the referee gives buyers that confidence for free. The lab-based (in vitro) Double Plate method is reported as highly reproducible between labs.
- **"The claims are physics" is wrong about the headline test.** SPF tested on volunteers (in vivo) is a biological test: a technician judges skin redness by eye. Only the in vitro method is purely optical.
- **The category pulls against the brand.** Facial SPF sells on texture and glow, and the brand's style rules ban glowing-skin photos ([philosophy](../../../docs/01-philosophy.md)). The texture would come from a contract manufacturer's base formula, which is where the Lean Screen and Purito failures both started. First Principle would own a disclosure policy, not the product.

Where the Skeptic goes too far:
- **Check 2 isn't failed; it's conditional.** It depends on price and batch size, not on physics.
  - In the Investor's table, it passes at €24.95 with 25,000-unit batches. It fails with 5,000-unit batches at the top lab price, and at €5.25 at any batch size.
  - Whether a brand with no texture and no name can charge €24.95 is a demand question.
  - The KILL rests on check 3, not on the stronger claim that two checks fail.
- **The legal point is a reading, not legal advice.** It would limit how the product is advertised, but it isn't what kills it.

### The Believer is right about the enemy, wrong about the gap

- **The enemy is real:** CHOICE's 16 of 20, Lean Screen at SPF 4, Purito at about SPF 19, and 5 of 23 children's sunscreens failing Warentest on UV protection this June.
- **Her evidence shows the referee working.** Warentest caught Junglück and i+m, and INCIDecoder's founder caught Purito. Her own line is the case against the idea: "Good sunscreen physics is already for sale: 8 of the 14 face creams … were rated 'gut'."
- **Two of her claims break on her own numbers:**
  - "Disclosure is the one thing incumbents can't copy cheaply." Her own figure, 1–3% of the price, says copying is cheap. Naked Sundays, Ultra Violette, Purito and Beauty of Joseon already do parts of it.
  - "The record also compounds." Every reformulation and every change of method resets it, and both are coming. The list of permitted UV filters keeps changing, and the industry is moving from ISO 24444 to ISO 23675.
- **Her best idea outlives the product:** a batch code that opens that batch's raw results. It's the Claims Log applied to each unit sold, and it fits any consumable First Principle makes next.

### The Investor sets the right bar, and nothing clears it

- €0 has been collected. There's no page, audience, formula or price.
- The contribution table is the most useful arithmetic in the file.
  - The numbers only work at about €25 a tube with 25,000-unit batches.
  - A batch that size needs 8,300–12,500 customers.
  - Each customer brings in €13–39 in year 1, less than most published costs of winning one.
- The recurring revenue the [business-model doc](../../../docs/05-business-model.md) asks for is thinner than the Believer's six to eight weeks per tube. At the amounts people actually apply, a customer buys one to three tubes a year (the Skeptic's estimate).
- The first non-refundable euro is at least 7 months away, more likely 18–20. The first lab round fails an honest "50+" formula 29–58% of the time.
- The one step I don't take from the Investor is the route back through pre-orders. The paper screen has already answered the question they would ask. Running them would cost €600–4,600, plus setting up a company to take the money, only to get the same answer.

### What's left standing

Nothing that makes a company. The remaining niche is buyers who:
- want more verification than Beauty of Joseon's results from two labs;
- won't take the referee's word;
- will pay about €25 to a brand with no texture and no name yet.

Nothing in the file shows that niche exists, let alone at a size that pays back €15–165k of launch cash.

Two things are worth keeping:
- **A practice:** the page behind each batch code, for whatever product comes next.
- **A lesson for the screen.** Both candidates hit the same wall. Their numbers were measurable, so referees and reviewers had already measured them, and a cheap product had already passed.
  - For air, that was the Luggable and Warentest's €100 purifiers. For sunscreen, it was Warentest's €5.25 face fluid.
  - The screen looks for categories where "the incumbents themselves overclaim a number that a standard test can measure". That points straight at the categories referees already cover.
  - So check 3 has to ask about the referee first.

## The 10-minute test

Every figure in this session came from search-result snippets, so check the one fact the KILL rests on yourself.

1. Open Stiftung Warentest's most recent face-sunscreen test (test.de, "Sonnencreme fürs Gesicht"). The full table may be behind a paywall.
2. List the SPF 50 and 50+ products it rated "gut", and check that each one met its label on UV protection.
3. Look up each one's price per 50 ml at dm or Rossmann today.

- **At least one costs less than €24.95,** the price the Investor's unit economics need: check 3 fails, and the KILL stands.
- **None does:** bring the list back, and the council reopens sunscreen.

## What would reopen it

There's no FIX FIRST change to make. A better testing plan improves the brand's own evidence without making it independent. Only the 10-minute test coming out the other way reopens sunscreen. A different headline number would be a new candidate, screened from check 1.

## Where the next session starts

- The project stays archived, and the website and Claims Log code stay frozen.
- Before the next candidate reaches the council, add referees to re-entry check 3.
  - Consumer testers that buy off the shelf (Warentest, Tænk, Which?, Öko-Test and the Euroconsumers groups) count as measured competitors.
  - The first question for any candidate becomes: has a referee already measured this number, and what's the cheapest product that passed?
- Then run that check first on shortlist #2, measured-comfort bedding: does a referee already publish a measured warmth figure for duvets that cost less? (I haven't checked.)
