# Idea brief: First Principle, verified-SPF facial sunscreen

Submitted to the council on 2026-09-28. Condensed from [the candidate screen](../../../docs/06-candidates.md) and the founder's research. Nothing in the plan is final.

## Where the project stands

- **The brand.** First Principle is an EU brand built against the wellness industry's pseudoscience. It only claims what it can measure, keeps a public Claims Log linking every claim to its evidence, and speaks in a calm, clinical tone.
- **The last product died on a measurement.** Its first product, a €400 air purifier, got FIX FIRST from this council on 2026-09-26. It then failed its first gate: the quietest fan found was 9.3 dB over the noise limit. See the [ledger](../../ledger.md).
- **The project is archived** until a category passes three re-entry checks on paper:
  1. the physics allow the claim;
  2. the physics allow a 50% gross margin at a price the market already pays;
  3. nobody already owns the number.

  Sunscreen is the first candidate to come through that screen: checks 1 and 2 pass on paper, check 3 only narrowly.

## The idea

A daily facial sunscreen sold on one verified number: its SPF.

- **The enemy.** In June 2025 the Australian consumer group CHOICE tested 20 sunscreens labelled SPF 50 or 50+, and 16 failed their label. Ultra Violette's Lean Screen SPF 50+ tested at SPF 4.
  - The maker's own retests at several labs ranged from SPF 4 to 64.
  - The base formula's manufacturer said it was unlikely to exceed SPF 21.
  - The regulator (the TGA) questioned the reliability of the lab that had certified it.
  - All 19 sunscreens built on the same base formula were cancelled from the Australian register and recalled.
- **The promise.** Test every formula at several independent labs (in vivo, ISO 24444), label it below the worst result, and publish every raw result for every batch. EU labels come in bands (a "50+" label needs a measured SPF of 60 or more), so labelling below the worst result is easy. Working line: *"Our label is lower than our worst test."*
- **Why lab variability matters.** ISO 24444 tests on at least 10 volunteers. The result depends on a technician reading skin redness by eye, on the volunteers' skin types and on small differences in application, so the same formula can score differently at different labs. The founder's view is that this variability is what consumer testers expose. Claude's view is that it explains SPF 40 versus 50, not 4 versus 50.
- **The claims are physics.** SPF and UVA protection are measured optical properties of the product. The brand claims nothing about skin health beyond what the label category means.

## Numbers so far (unverified)

- **One round of in vivo SPF testing:** $2,700–4,500 (founder's research). Spread over a 25,000-unit batch, that's 11–18 cents per unit, or about 30–55 cents with three labs.
- **Formula development:** $5,000–50,000 (founder's research).
- **Minimum order at a contract manufacturer:** around 25,000 units per formula (founder's research). Smaller EU manufacturers may go lower; nothing has been quoted yet.
- **Cost to make one unit:** €2–4 (Claude's estimate, not quoted).
- **Launch cash:** roughly €65–165k for development, testing and the first minimum order (Claude's estimate).
- **Price:** not set.

## Customer and market

- **First customer:** skincare-literate buyers who read ingredient lists and test results. This replaces the homelab engineers targeted for the purifier.
- **Market:** EU-wide and English-first, starting with DACH and the Nordics (carried over from the purifier plan). Italy has a large cosmetics contract-manufacturing sector.
- **Regulation:** in the EU a sunscreen is a cosmetic. It needs a safety assessment, a responsible person and notification, and the SPF test methods are set out in a 2006 Commission Recommendation.

## Known competition

- Beauty of Joseon, hugely popular with skincare enthusiasts, already publishes SPF results from two labs (52.5 ± 5.8 and 63.1 ± 0.6).
- EltaMD and Colorescience publish UVA and photostability data, and at least one brand says it tests every batch after launch.
- What seems unowned: testing every batch at several labs, publishing every raw result, and setting the label below the worst one.

## Known open questions

- Whether buyers pay for a verified number over a bigger printed one: "our 30" against someone else's "50+".
- Whether honest incumbents copy the idea once it matters.
- Real costs: lab quotes, minimum orders, unit cost.
- Formulation: who formulates it, which UV filters, and the texture, which is what facial sunscreens sell on.
- Price, sales channels and seasonality.
