# 1. The Believer

Council session 2026-09-28 · [Idea brief](idea.md)

## Who needs it

She's a skincare-literate buyer in Germany or Denmark who wears facial SPF every day and reads ingredient lists and test tables. She can't measure SPF herself, so she buys by proxy: this year's Stiftung Warentest grade, a pharmacy brand, or a K-beauty tube with a lab report. Those proxies fail at every price:
- **Germany.** In 2025 Warentest rated two face creams "mangelhaft": Junglück at €36 and i+m at €20.90. This June it failed five of 23 children's sunscreens on UV protection, Bübchen among them.
- **Denmark.** Tænk lists 3 of 16 facial sunscreens as failing. Only 1 of 7 "SPF 50+" tubes it bought on Temu, Shein and AliExpress met its claim.

When the proxies disagree, she has nothing to decide with. i+m answered Warentest with its own ISO 24444 result and called the referee's method controversial.

The most committed buyers pay to find out. In 2020 the founder of INCIDecoder noticed only two UV filters on Purito's "SPF 50+" ingredient list. She paid for tests at labs in Poland and Germany, and both measured about SPF 19. That set off recalls or sales stops at Purito and at four other brands supplied by the same manufacturer.

## Why now

Three things changed in the last two years.
- **The method.** Two new standards were published in December 2024: ISO 23675 (fully in vitro) and ISO 23698 (HDRS, no sunburn).
  - Cosmetics Europe told its members in June 2025 to phase them in as preferred alternatives to ISO 24444.
  - SGS launched commercial HDRS testing that October.
  - In a five-lab ring study, the in vitro method was highly reproducible between labs, while in vivo results vary widely.

  Retesting every batch no longer means sunburning a fresh panel of volunteers.
- **The referee.** Stiftung Warentest grades with ISO 23698. EU labels, by the German industry association IKW's own account, rest almost entirely on ISO 24444. So brands certified one way are judged the other way, in public, every summer. Warentest notes that brands that did well in earlier years go on to fail.
- **The trust shock.** After CHOICE found 16 of 20 below their label, Australia's regulator (the TGA) proposed periodic testing and lab oversight in March 2026. Ultra Violette now tests across several labs, and its relaunched mineral Velvet Screen says SPF 40 where the earlier version said 50. The brand hurt most by the scandal now sells a smaller number.

Demand is moving the same way. Prestige face sun care in Europe grew 26% in the year to March 2026 (Circana), and people now buy it as daily skincare rather than for the beach.

## The best version

**The product.** A daily facial fluid on modern EU filters. It starts from a contract manufacturer's proven base but trusts none of its paperwork, because Purito's and Lean Screen's failures both spread from one manufacturer's base formula to a whole shelf of brands.

**The testing.** Before launch, two or three unrelated labs test the formula in vivo, and it's also tested by HDRS. HDRS is Warentest's method, and it reports SPF and UVA protection in one test. Every batch is retested before release, and a batch that tests below its band doesn't ship.

**The page.** The batch code on the tube opens a page with every raw result, lab, method and date, and the worst result shown next to the label.

**Why it works.** The rule holds whoever is right about Australia, the founder or Claude:
- labelling below the worst result absorbs lab noise;
- no single lab can carry the label;
- the optical method takes the technician's eye out of the reading.

**Why a smaller number costs little.** SPF 50 lets through 2% of burning UV and SPF 60 lets through 1.7%. Lean Screen, measured at SPF 4, let through 25%, more than twelve times as much as a real SPF 50.

**The recurring revenue.** Sunscreen is the recurring revenue the business-model doc says hardware lacks. At the dose the test assumes, a 50 ml tube lasts six to eight weeks (my estimate).

**If it goes right,** the next Warentest or Tænk result lands inside the published range and goes into the Claims Log whatever it says. First Principle becomes the brand journalists call after the next failed test table.

## The unfair advantage

First Principle doesn't need to out-engineer anyone, only to out-disclose them. The purifier had to beat a £251 box on physics. Good sunscreen physics is already for sale: 8 of the 14 face creams in Warentest's 2025 test were rated "gut".

Disclosure is the one thing incumbents can't copy cheaply, and the barrier isn't the lab bill. Three in vivo labs per batch cost roughly 1–3% of a €20–36 price, the range of the two face creams Warentest failed (estimate). The barrier is each rival's existing position:
- **Ultra Violette** now tests across several labs, but it hasn't said whether it will publish the reports. Its argument is that SPF data can be misread out of context.
- **Beauty of Joseon**, the most transparent rival, publishes 52.5 ± 5.8 from a Korean lab and 63.1 ± 0.6 from a Spanish lab, for a sunscreen sold as SPF50+. Under EU bands "50+" needs a measured 60, so labelling below its worst result would make it print 50.
- **Natural brands** like Junglück and i+m sell zinc-only formulas. Warentest's testers said zinc oxide alone makes reliable protection "apparently very difficult to impossible". These brands can't switch to the filters that measure best without contradicting their own pitch; natural-cosmetics standards generally allow only mineral filters (not checked here). First Principle bans "chemical-free" from its own copy and can choose filters purely by measurement.

The record also compounds. A rival can start publishing tomorrow, but it can't backfill three years of batches.

## The bet

Trust has become the scarce part of an SPF. After Purito, CHOICE and Warentest, the skincare-literate buyer will choose, and pay a premium for, the tube that shows every batch's worst result over a bigger number backed only by a brand's word.

Concretely, the first formula has to meet two conditions:
- clear 50 in its worst test across labs and methods, with room for batches to vary;
- feel good enough to wear every day.

Then the shelf choice is "a 50 that passed every test" against "a 50+ that passed one", never "our 30" against "their 50+".
