# 3. The Investor

Council session 2026-09-26 · [Idea brief](idea.md) · [Believer's case](1-believer.md) · [Skeptic's attack](2-skeptic.md)

## Proof people will pay: none

- **Money collected so far is €0.** The docs show no deposits, pre-orders, waitlist or letters of intent. There's no prototype to pay for and no unit cost to set a price against.
- **All of the Believer's demand evidence shows interest, not payment:**
  - GitHub stars (188 and 144) on replacement firmware, and 7 and 4 on the Flipper apps. These are people hacking €100–200 machines and avoiding paying for filters.
  - 2 million Home Assistant installs. That's a population, not a list of buyers.
  - IQAir at €1,399 (the Believer's figure; I didn't verify it) and Austin Air. Those are other brands' customers.
- **The payment evidence I could find points the other way:**
  - **The pitch closest to this one raised little.** Smart Air sells purifiers on data and myth-busting. Its Sqair Kickstarter in 2019 drew 210 backers and HK$248,172 (about $32k), at $85–105 per unit.
  - **The big purifier raise told the opposite story.** Mila sold design, an app and a filter subscription. It raised $1,156,992 from 3,636 backers in 2019, about $318 each.
  - **The engineer-skeptic's favourite air brand sells cheap units.** AirGradient (open source, works with Home Assistant) says it has 30,000+ monitors deployed, priced at $138–230.
  - **Mainstream buyers pay under €200.** Stiftung Warentest's 5/2026 issue kept the Bosch Air 4000 (300 m³/h) as test winner. The Trotec 170 E (350 m³/h) lists at €149.99.
  - **Quiet clean air already sells for less.** Intertek rates the Luggable XL-7 at 259 CFM for smoke (440 m³/h). HouseFresh measured it at 38.8 dB(A). It costs about £251 in the UK.

## When the first real euro arrives

| Money | When | Why |

| Best case | About 6 months (estimate) | Requires no radio (so no Matter or radio cybersecurity testing), catalogue parts, a sheet-metal box and Germany only |

- **The wood-smoke season is already lost.** It starts next month. The first winter this box can sell into is 2027/28.
- **Cash spent before that first euro: €130–370k (estimate).** None of it is budgeted.
  - engineering and prototypes: €30–80k
  - safety, EMC (electrical interference), CADR and ozone tests: €20–50k
  - Matter, if wanted: $19–23k ($7k membership, $2k certification, $10–14k lab)
  - tooling: €5–30k
  - WEEE and packaging registrations: €3–10k
  - a first run of 300–500 units: €75–175k

## Why each sale earns so little

- **Net price after VAT:** €336 in Germany, €320 in Denmark and Sweden.
- **Landed cost per unit: €190–500, with a realistic first run at €250–350 (estimate).** The Skeptic's ceiling for a 50% gross margin is €168.
  - H13 287 × 592 × 292 mm filter module: €108–173 for one unit at German B2B shops, €50–90 at volume (estimate).
  - EC fan: the ebm-papst R3G190 is €291 for one unit. At volume it's €120–180, or €40–80 for a Chinese equivalent (estimates).
  - 2.3 kg of coconut carbon: €3–10 at bulk prices of $1.5–4.5/kg. The headline differentiator is the cheapest part in the box and the easiest to copy.
- **Costs after the factory:** fulfilment, a 15–20 kg parcel, payment fees, returns and the 2-year legal guarantee add €30–80 per unit (estimate).
- **Contribution per unit (what's left after those costs): −€260 to +€120. For a realistic first run it's −€110 to +€55, roughly zero (estimate).**
- **Paid advertising doesn't fit in that margin.** LaunchBoom's figures (over $20 per $1 reservation, 35–45% of reservations converting) work out to about $45–60 per buyer (derived estimate). Customers have to come free, from the tear-down videos, and house rule 4 makes those videos show the Luggable beating this box.
- **Filter revenue is about €0.** The filter is a standard module that anyone sells, and it loads slowly because it runs at a quarter of its rated flow. A carbon refill is €3–10 of commodity material.
- **The first customer group has a ceiling.** The Skeptic's ~5,000 engineer buyers (estimate) × €400 is €2.0M of sales, once. At €0–80 per unit that's €0–400k of contribution, against €60–190k of launch cost before inventory. The niche can pay back the launch. It can't fund a company.

## The cheapest demand test this week

Take refundable deposits, not a waitlist. Newsletter signups convert to buyers at 5–10%, and even $1 reservations convert at only 35–45% (LaunchBoom). Total cost is about €600, or under €100 without ads.

1. **Day 1 (under €100):** put up one page on the Shopify back end already chosen. Take a €50 deposit against a €400 price, refundable on request at any time. Claim only what is true today: the price, a standard H13 module, 2.3 kg of refillable carbon, no app and no account. Make no CADR claim.
2. **Show the chart that beats you.** The chart shows:
   - Luggable XL-7: 440 m³/h at 38.8 dB(A), about £251
   - Trotec 170 E: 350 m³/h, €100–150
   - Bosch Air 4000: 300 m³/h

   Next to it, promise: "If our unit doesn't beat the Luggable on CADR at equal noise, in the same room on the same meter, every deposit comes back." A deposit placed next to that chart is real demand. One placed without it will disappear in the first Reddit thread.
3. **Days 2–7 (€500):** post on r/homeassistant, r/homelab, r/AirPurifiers, the Home Assistant forum and Show HN, plus €500 of Reddit ads in Germany, Austria, the Netherlands, Denmark, Sweden and Finland. The target is 2,000–3,000 visitors (estimate). The heating season is starting, so the smoke problem will be fresh in buyers' minds, which favours the idea.
4. **Day 7:**
   - under 30 deposits: stop.
   - 30–100: keep the page up until day 30.
   - over 100: build the Skeptic's plywood prototype and get landed-cost quotes.

## Would I put my own money in?

**No.** There's €0 of paid demand, no unit cost and no prototype. At €400 each unit makes roughly zero, and €130–370k has to be spent before the first euro comes back.

**The number that would change my mind: 300 paid €50 deposits within 30 days, collected on the page with the chart that shows the Luggable and the Trotec.**
- That's €120k of orders, roughly one first production run (estimate).
- It's 1.4× the backers Smart Air got for the same data-first pitch, at about 5× the price.
- If that pace held for a year (about 3,600 units), it would pay back €60–190k of launch cost at the €50–80 per unit a cost-down would have to reach (estimate).

At 300, I'd fund the prototype and supplier quotes (€20–30k, estimate), not inventory.
